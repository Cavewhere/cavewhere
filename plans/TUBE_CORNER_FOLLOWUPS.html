T07ad — a corner's rings sample the two legs' walls

Case 30 at s1: a 60° turn, the passage 2.5 m wide each side at the inset ring before the corner, 3 m at s1, 2.7 m at the inset ring after. Today the corner ring puts the 3 m reading on the bisector at face value (1), which is only 3·cos 30° = 2.6 m from either leg's centerline, and the two elbow rings beside it lerp radii between rings whose centers are 0.5 m apart (2): the outside wall bulges 0.3 m, the inside wall gets a 0.1 m shelf. After: each leg's wall runs at its interpolated width right up to the bisector plane, and every elbow ring (the middle one included) reaches from its own center to that envelope. The outside corner then sits where the two leg walls meet, 3.46 m from s1, the inside corner where they cross.

The same at Phake a2, as a graph along the passage: the inside wall's distance from the centerline, from the a1→a2 inset ring through the elbow to the a2→a3 inset ring. The legs interpolate toward 2.5 at a2; the corner delivers 2.5·cos 28.5° = 2.2, so the wall dips where you expect its peak. That dip is the notch you saw; the 2.5 m section spoke beside it is why the section looks wider than the rings.

T07ae — a leg and its end station use the same reading

Phake a4 is read in two chunks: L0 R2 U2 D1.7 in a1–a5 (facing on toward a5) and L5 R0 U3 D23 in a4–a6 (facing a6). Today the leg a3→a4 interpolates toward the first record, so its left wall runs 1.5 → 0, while the a4 station ring takes the widest of both records (L = 5) and, because the biggest reading sets the elbow's inset, that 5 m starts 3.5 m before a4: fat, skinny, fat. After: a chain leg and its end ring use the record whose facing lies along that leg; the other record belongs to the a4→a6 passage and its mouth. The a3→a4 left wall then runs 1.5 → 0 all the way, and the 5 m wall shows up only on a4→a6.
