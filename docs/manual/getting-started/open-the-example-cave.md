# Open the Example Cave

## Why / when you need this

A fresh install opens on an empty cave, which makes a hard place to learn from.
Nothing to orbit, no trips to open, no notes to look at. Before you have surveyed
anything of your own, the fastest way to see what CaveWhere does is to open a
cave someone has already built.

**Phake Cave 3000** is that cave: a complete, multi-trip example project whose
scraps are already morphed onto a 3D model. This manual's screenshots all come
from it, so opening it gives you the same data the rest of these pages describe.
See below.

![A 3D view of Phake Cave 3000: hand-drawn passage sketches morphed and carpeted over the red survey line, its two levels and the vertical connection between them visible in three dimensions.](../images/scraps-carpet-orbit-poster.png)
*Phake Cave 3000 once you have downloaded it and started orbiting.*

## On macOS and Windows: click to open

On macOS and Windows, one click downloads the example cave and opens it:

**[▶ Open the Example Cave](https://cavewhere.com/open?repo=https://github.com/Cavewhere/PhakeCave3000.git)**

Your browser hands the link to CaveWhere, which pops up the **Clone Repository**
dialog described below. Installing CaveWhere on those 2 platforms
[registers the `cavewhere://` link scheme](install-cavewhere.md), so the link
works anywhere it appears: this page, an email, a web page. Share it with anyone
who has CaveWhere installed.

## On Linux: paste the link into the app

On Linux, clicking the link does not reach CaveWhere, so you hand the cave over
yourself:

1. In CaveWhere, open **File → Open from Link…** (on Linux the **File** menu sits
   at the top of the sidebar).
2. Paste the repository URL:

   ```
   https://github.com/Cavewhere/PhakeCave3000.git
   ```
3. Click **Open**. CaveWhere opens the same **Clone Repository** dialog.

## Download and open

However you got here, CaveWhere shows a **Clone Repository** dialog naming the
repository (`github.com / Cavewhere/PhakeCave3000`) with a **Destination** folder
below it. The destination decides where the downloaded cave lands on your disk.
Click the path to choose a different folder, or leave the default alone.

The example repository is public, so **you do not need to sign in**. Leave the
**Account** picker alone. Signing in only matters for private team repositories;
see [Sign In to GitHub](../collaboration/sign-in-to-github.md).

Click **Clone & Open**. A progress bar tracks the download, and CaveWhere opens
Phake Cave 3000 when it finishes. Because CaveWhere
[holds one project at a time](../projects-and-files/open-a-project.md), it first
asks what to do with whatever project you already have open, exactly as **Open**
does.

The cave arrives as an ordinary project on your disk. Orbit it, edit it, break
it; none of that matters. Your changes stay on your machine and never reach the
shared copy online unless you deliberately sync. I recommend breaking it on
purpose: change a shot distance and watch the loop closure shift and the scraps
re-carpet to follow. Nothing you do here can damage anyone
else's copy.
